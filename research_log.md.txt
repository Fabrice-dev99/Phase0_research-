# Research Log – Digital Skill Connection

| # | Source (APA) | Type | Link | Key finding | Gap it shows |
|---|--------------|------|------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda. (2025). *Labour Force Survey Annual Report 2024*. NISR. | Report / Statistics | https://www.statistics.gov.rw | Informal employment accounts for about 90.4% of total employment in Rwanda. Many skilled workers operate outside formal systems. | There is no trusted digital platform that formalizes and connects informal skilled workers (plumbers, electricians, mechanics, etc.) with customers. |
| 2 | United Nations Development Programme. (2026). *Youth Employment Brief: Analysis of Skills Matching with Private Sector Needs*. UNDP Rwanda. | Report | https://www.undp.org/rwanda | Rwanda faces a significant skills mismatch. Employers report gaps in practical and soft skills, and many youth remain underemployed. | Existing systems do not effectively match verified practical skills of workers with real customer demand in the service sector. |
| 3 | World Bank. (2026). *Rwanda’s Skills Training Delivers Jobs and Opportunity for Youth*. World Bank. | Report | https://www.worldbank.org | Skills training improves employment outcomes, but many youth still struggle to find work due to weak linkages between training and the labour market. | Training alone is not enough; a marketplace that connects trained skilled workers to customers is missing. |
| 4 | Rani, U., & Furrer, M. (2021). Digital labour platforms and new forms of flexible work in developing countries: Algorithmic management of work and workers. *Competition & Change*, 25(2), 212–236. | Research Paper | https://journals.sagepub.com | Digital platforms can create work opportunities in developing countries but often lack transparency and worker protection. | Most platforms focus on online freelancing; very few focus on local physical skilled services (plumbing, electrical, cleaning, etc.) in African contexts. |
| 5 | Anwar, M. A. (2022). Platforms of inequality: Gender dynamics of digital labour in Africa. *Gender & Development*, 30(3), 747–764. | Research Paper | https://www.tandfonline.com | Digital labour platforms in Africa can create new inequalities and often fail to protect vulnerable workers. | There is a need for platforms designed specifically for local skilled trades that build trust through verification, ratings, and fair payment systems. |
| 6 | Ashraf, M. (2026). *Home Service Customer Satisfaction* [Dataset]. Kaggle. | Dataset | https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mohamed444ashraf/rating-predicition | Large dataset (300,000 records) containing service type, technician performance, response time, and customer ratings. | Real service marketplace data exists, but Rwanda lacks a local platform that uses similar structured data for matching and trust-building. |
| 7 | Oracle. (n.d.). *Oracle Database PL/SQL Language Reference*. Oracle Corporation. | Technical Source | https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/oracle-database/ | PL/SQL supports procedures, functions, cursors, packages, and triggers that can enforce business rules and automate processes. | Technical capability exists to build secure, rule-based matching, payment, and rating systems, but it has not been applied to a local skilled services marketplace in Rwanda. |
